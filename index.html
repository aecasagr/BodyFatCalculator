⚠ Always Think About the Whole Person First
These numbers are a starting point for a conversation — not a prescription. Always look at the full picture before saying anything about weight.

For older or more fragile patients, telling them to lose weight can actually do more harm than good. Losing weight at the wrong time can mean losing muscle, feeling weaker, and increasing the risk of falls or illness. A slightly higher weight is often perfectly fine — and sometimes even protective — in older adults. Use your judgement. The numbers are a guide, not the goal.

⚠ Remember — Numbers Don't Tell the Whole Story
The weight targets above are just estimates based on averages. Before bringing up weight with a patient, think about how they're actually doing day-to-day — their age, how mobile they are, any other health conditions, and what matters most to them. For older or frailer patients, pushing for weight loss can backfire. If you're not sure, talk it through with the rest of the team. The patient's wellbeing always comes first.
